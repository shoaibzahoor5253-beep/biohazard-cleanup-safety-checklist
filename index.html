When is Professional Remediation Appropriate?

Biological materials pose serious infectious, chemical, and structural risks. In the following situations, property owners and managers should avoid touching or disturbing affected materials and immediately contact licensed biohazard remediation specialists:

Blood or Bodily Fluids

Trauma, severe injuries, industrial incidents, or medical events where blood, bodily fluids, or tissue may contain bloodborne pathogens (e.g. Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, HIV).

Suspected Biological Contamination

Category 3 blackwater or sewage backups, infectious disease exposures, or unknown toxic organic matter permeating flooring, subsurfaces, or HVAC systems.

Unattended Death Scenes

Scenes where natural decomposition produces hazardous biological fluids, intense odors, and porous surface permeation that require specialized enzymatic remediation.

Hazardous Biological Materials

Heavy animal or rodent infestations (Hantavirus risks), hoarded biohazard materials, or chemical-biological cross-contamination requiring regulated medical waste transport.

Situations Where Occupants are Unsure How to Assess the Risk

Whenever landlords, tenants, or facility managers cannot definitively confirm the non-hazardous nature of a biological substance, treat it as hazardous. Erring on the side of caution prevents widespread cross-contamination and liability.

Crucial Safety Advisory: Under no circumstances should property owners attempt DIY remediation of serious biological hazards. Biological contamination requires certified containment barriers, negative air pressure, hospital-grade EPA-registered disinfectants, and manifest-tracked biohazard waste disposal.
